# Sources

Every technique in framework.md, cold-start.md and practice-habits.md cites one of these.
**[research]** = peer-reviewed / academic. **[practitioner]** = interviewer and coach consensus.

- **S2** [practitioner] Meta Careers, "Preparing for your software engineering interview at Meta" (2022) — https://www.metacareers.com/blog/preparing-for-your-software-engineering-interview-at-meta/ — 35-minute coding block in shared editor or whiteboard, problems explainable in a few minutes, "If you are stuck, ask questions".
- **S3** [practitioner] Dean Jackson (Google engineer; byline per R3), "The Google Technical Interview" (2013) — https://static.googleusercontent.com/media/research.google.com/en/us/pubs/archive/41881.pdf — Clarify assumptions first, interviewer withholds info until asked, three model clarifications, recheck assumptions when stuck, brute force fine, brief silence OK, fishing is bad.
- **S4** [practitioner] Amazon (aboutamazon.com), "Amazon job interview for software development engineer: Recruiter tips" (2024) — https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/workplace/amazon-job-interview-software-development-engineer-recruiter-tips — Recruiter-voice advice to ask clarifying questions as a breather that avoids misunderstandings; edge cases and bad input as themes.
- **S5** [practitioner] Gayle Laakmann McDowell, "Cracking the Coding Interview, 4th ed." (2008) — http://www.r-5.org/files/books/computers/overviews/cs-tools/Gayle_Laakmann-Cracking_the_Coding_Interview-EN.pdf — Step 1 is resolving ambiguity; many interviewers (especially Microsoft) test whether you ask good questions.
- **S6** [practitioner] Gayle Laakmann McDowell, "Cracking the Coding Skills v6" (n.d.) — https://www.crackingthecodinginterview.com/uploads/6/5/2/8/6528028/cracking_the_coding_skills_-_v6.pdf — Seven steps (Listen, Example, Brute force, Optimize, Walk through, Implement, Test) with large-generic-example, solve-by-hand and do-not-solve-in-head heuristics.
- **S8** [practitioner] Karat, "Tech Interviews with Karat" (2022) — https://communications.karat.com/hubfs/For_Candidates/Tech%20Interviews%20with%20Karat_2022.pdf — Per-problem flow includes an opportunity to ask clarifying questions before describing an algorithm; candidates judged primarily on solving.
- **S10** [practitioner] Karat, "How to hire great software engineers with interview rubrics" (2021) — https://karat.com/interview-engineering-how-to-create-a-structured-rubric-for-technical-interviews/ — Lists presence or absence of clarifying questions among noisy indicators to ignore (verified 2026-10-07); off-rubric competencies are noise.
- **S12** [practitioner] Karat, "Human + AI Technical Interview Rubrics" (2026) — https://karat.com/resource/human-ai-technical-interview-rubrics/ — Observable signals include asking multiple relevant questions about goals/requirements and making reasonable assumptions.
- **S14** [practitioner] Yangshun Tay / Tech Interview Handbook, "Coding interview cheatsheet" (n.d.) — https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/coding-interview-cheatsheet/ — Checklist: paraphrase, clarify assumptions/range/format, at least 2–3 questions, simplified example, wait for green light.
- **S15** [practitioner] Ian Douglas / interviewing.io, "I've conducted over 600 technical interviews on interviewing.io. Here are 5 common problem areas I've seen" (2020) — https://interviewing.io/blog/ive-conducted-over-600-technical-interviews-on-interviewing-io-here-are-5-common-problem-areas-ive-seen — Not asking clarifying questions is a common failure; set a think-quietly-then-share contract; ask for help rather than struggle silently.
- **S16** [practitioner] Dima Korolev / interviewing.io, "Does communication matter in technical interviewing? We looked at 100K interviews to find out" (2022) — https://interviewing.io/blog/does-communication-matter-in-technical-interviewing-we-looked-at-100k-interviews-to-find-out — Communicate score predicts advancement far less than Solve or Code (4-4-2 advanced 96%).
- **S17** [practitioner] Aline Lerner / interviewing.io, "We analyzed thousands of technical interviews on everything from language to code style" (2017) — https://interviewing.io/blog/we-analyzed-thousands-of-technical-interviews-on-everything-from-language-to-code-style-here-s-what-we-found — Having seen the question raised hirability 16.6%; successful candidates ran code slightly later.
- **S18** [practitioner] Aline Lerner / interviewing.io, "The technical interview practice gap" (2021) — https://interviewing.io/blog/technical-interview-practice-gap — 5+ practice interviews associated with roughly 2× FAANG pass odds; few perform consistently.
- **S19** [practitioner] Hello Interview, "Low Level Design Interview Delivery Framework" (n.d.) — https://www.hellointerview.com/learn/low-level-design/in-a-hurry/delivery — Timed LLD opening with four requirement themes (capabilities, rules, errors, scope) and a fixed sequence claimed to ground nerves.
- **S20** [practitioner] Hello Interview, "Rate Limiter Low Level Design" (n.d.) — https://www.hellointerview.com/learn/low-level-design/problem-breakdowns/rate-limiter — Worked OOD clarifying dialogue ending in a written requirements and out-of-scope list.
- **S21** [practitioner] Stefan Mai / Hello Interview, "System Design Requirements Gathering" (n.d.) — https://www.hellointerview.com/blog/system-design-requirements — Requirements gathering is the #1 mid-level mock feedback; time-box ~5 min; over-inquiry is a rabbit hole; quantify NFRs.
- **S22** [practitioner] Exponent, "Design a Rate Limiter" (n.d.) — https://www.tryexponent.com/courses/system-design-interviews-v2/design-rate-limiter — Rate-limiter clarifying categories (key, deny behaviour, strictness, placement) and level tied to depth of reasoning.
- **S23** [practitioner] intervu.dev, "LRU Cache interview walkthrough" (2026) — https://intervu.dev/blog/walkthroughs/lru-cache-interview-walkthrough/ — LRU clarifications (miss return value, put-as-use, capacity ≥ 1, thread safety), matching Senior Engineer Academy and PracHub lists.
- **S24** [practitioner] The DSA Handbook, "The first five minutes: clarifying questions" (2026) — https://dsa.handbook.academy/curriculum/interview-framework/clarifying-questions/ — "Does the answer change the code?" filter, question order, 3–5 min budget, and failure modes (ask-then-ignore, fishing, stalling); its interviewer-notes citation is overstated.
- **S25** [practitioner] IGotAnOffer, "Coding interview questions" (n.d.) — https://igotanoffer.com/blogs/tech/coding-interview-questions — Four-step Clarify/Plan/Implement/Test with clarify spanning minutes 0–10.
- **S27** [practitioner] Santosh Rout / InterviewNode, "How to prepare for interviews where the problem statement is intentionally incomplete" (2026) — https://interviewnode.com/post/how-to-prepare-for-interviews-where-the-problem-statement-is-intentionally-incomplete — Over-clarification without direction reads as stalling; state material assumptions and move forward.
- **S29** [practitioner] CertoJob, "Whiteboard Interview Strategies" (2026) — https://certojob.com/interviews/technical-interviews/whiteboard-interview-strategies — Whiteboard layout with examples, approach, edge cases and implementation regions; restate before writing.
- **S30** [practitioner] SpaceComplexity, "Ask These Clarifying Questions First. The Algorithm Will Follow" (2026) — https://spacecomplexity.ai/blog/clarifying-questions-coding-interview — Maps each clarifying question to an algorithm consequence; warns against reciting a memorised checklist.
- **S33** [practitioner] Fullstack Academy, "Whiteboard Coding Interviews: 6 Steps to Solve Any Problem" (2019) — https://www.fullstackacademy.com/blog/whiteboard-coding-interviews-a-6-step-process-to-solve-any-problem — REACTO targets the nervous reflex to start writing and advises not announcing the framework.
- **S34** [practitioner] interviewing.io (anonymous Meta interviewer), "Python Interview with a Meta engineer" (2025) — https://interviewing.io/mocks/facebook-python-closest-pair-sum — Ask at least two questions, one-sentence brute force, write plan steps against stress-induced forgetting; companion debrief warns over-polished pseudocode eats time.
- **S36** [practitioner] Atul Gawande (reporting Daniel Boorman, Boeing), "The Checklist Manifesto" (2009) — https://www.chestergrant.com/highlights-from-the-checklist-manifesto-by-atul-gawande — Checklist rules: pause point, Read-Do vs Do-Confirm, 5–9 killer items, 60–90 s before it becomes a distraction.
- **S38** [research] Behroozi, Shirolkar, Barik, Parnin, "Does Stress Impact Technical Interview Performance?" (2020) — https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3368089.3409712 — RCT (n=48): being watched at the whiteboard cut performance by more than half and raised stress and cognitive load.
- **S39** [research] Ford, Barik, Rand-Pickett, Parnin, "The Tech-Talk Balance: What Technical Interviewers Expect from Technical Candidates" (2017) — https://denaeford.me/papers/tech-talk-balance-CHASE-2017.pdf — Interviewers from 9 companies (70 forms) valued relevant questions and disliked evasive answers in mock interviews.
- **S40** [research] Behroozi et al., "Debugging Hiring: What Went Right and What Went Wrong in the Technical Interview Process" (2020) — https://static.barik.net/barik/publications/icse2020/behroozi_icseseis2020.pdf — Candidates report vague prompts and interviewers reacting badly to clarifying questions.
- **S43** [research] Atman, Adams, Cardella, Turns, Mosborg, Saleem, "Engineering Design Processes: A Comparison of Students and Expert Practitioners" (2007) — https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/j.2168-9830.2007.tb00945.x — Experts spent more time scoping and gathered more information across more categories than students.
- **S45** [research] Kenzi, Soffer, Hadar, "The Role of Domain Knowledge in Requirements Elicitation: An Exploratory Study" (2010) — https://aisel.aisnet.org/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1048&context=mcis2010 — Domain-familiar analysts skip trivial questions and miss tacit assumptions.
- **S46** [research] Aranda, Dieste, Panach, Juristo, "Effect of Requirements Analyst Experience on Elicitation Effectiveness: A Family of Quasi-Experiments" (2024) — https://exa.ai/library/publication/7qzkz52067d — 12 quasi-experiments (124 subjects): professional experience had a moderate negative effect in familiar domains.
- **S47** [research] Bano et al., "Learning from Mistakes: An Empirical Study of Elicitation Interviews Performed by Novices" (2018) — https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10105610 — Novice elicitors omit expected questions and ask vague ones, and did not improve across three interviews without feedback.
- **S48** [research] Moran, "Anxiety and working memory capacity: a meta-analysis and narrative review" (2016) — https://psycnet.apa.org/record/2016-11916-001 — Anxiety reduces working-memory performance (g = −.334, N = 22,061).
- **S49** [research] Beilock & Carr, "When High-Powered People Fail: Working Memory and 'Choking Under Pressure' in Math" (2005) — https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/psychological-science/j.0956-7976.2005.00789.x/ — Pressure harms high-WM individuals on high-WM-demand problems.
- **S50** [research] Beilock & Carr, "On the fragility of skilled performance: What governs choking under pressure?" (2001) — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11757876/ — Choking via distraction (WM loss) vs explicit monitoring depending on proceduralisation.
- **S51** [research] Eysenck, Derakshan, Santos, Calvo, "Anxiety and cognitive performance: Attentional control theory" (2007) — https://eprints.bbk.ac.uk/id/eprint/3950/ — Anxiety impairs inhibition and shifting and costs efficiency more than accuracy.
- **S52** [research] Jamieson, Mendes, Blackstock, Schmader, "Turning the knots in your stomach into bows: Reappraising arousal improves performance on the GRE" (2010) — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20161454/ — Arousal reappraisal raised GRE-math scores in a small randomised study (n ≈ 28).
- **S53** [research] Jamieson, Nock, Mendes, "Mind over matter: Reappraising arousal improves cardiovascular and cognitive responses to stress" (2012) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3410434/ — Reappraisal produced a challenge cardiovascular profile and less threat bias than an ignore instruction.
- **S54** [research] Brooks, "Get Excited: Reappraising Pre-Performance Anxiety as Excitement" (2014) — https://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/releases/xge-a0035325.pdf — Reframing anxiety as excitement beat calming down on math (d = .39, n ≈ 220) and other tasks.
- **S55** [research] Poynter & Pasqualini, "Getting Excited About Public Speaking: A Replication" (2025) — https://doi.org/10.58734/plc-2025-0011 — Direct replication reproduced self-reported excitement but found no difference in observer-rated performance.
- **S56** [research] Ramirez & Beilock, "Writing about testing worries boosts exam performance in the classroom" (2011) — https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1199427 — Original small-sample claim that pre-test expressive writing eliminated choking.
- **S57** [research] O'Meara & Lovett, "Single-session expressive writing interventions for test anxiety: a meta-analysis" (2026) — https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/10615806.2025.2552857 — 21 studies (N = 1,457) fail to support single-session expressive writing.
- **S58** [research] Gollwitzer & Sheeran, "Implementation intentions and goal achievement: a meta-analysis of effects and processes" (2006) — https://www.socmot.uni-konstanz.de/publications/implementation-intentions-and-goal-achievement-meta-analysis-effects-and-processes — If-then plans improve goal attainment (d = .65, 94 tests), strongest on initiating action.
- **S59** [research] Parks-Stamm, Gollwitzer, Oettingen, "Implementation intentions and test anxiety: shielding academic performance from distraction" (2010) — https://www.socmot.uni-konstanz.de/sites/default/files/10_Parks_Stamm_Gollwitzer_Implementation_Intentions_Test_Anxiety.pdf — For high-anxious students, ignore-distraction plans helped and focus-harder plans hurt.
- **S62** [research] Risko & Gilbert, "Cognitive Offloading" (2016) — https://www.cell.com/trends/cognitive-sciences/fulltext/S1364-6613(16)30098-5 — Offloading to the environment lowers internal demand, and people misjudge when to use it.
- **S63** [research] Loksa & Ko, "The role of self-regulation in programming problem solving process and success" (2016) — https://faculty.washington.edu/ajko/papers/Loksa2016SelfRegulation.pdf — Novices rarely reinterpret prompts before coding; planning and comprehension monitoring tracked fewer errors.
- **S64** [research] Loksa, Ko, Jernigan, Oleson, Mendez, Burnett, "Programming, Problem Solving, and Self-Awareness: Effects of Explicit Guidance" (2016) — https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2858036.2858252 — Explicit staged problem-solving guidance raised productivity, independence, self-efficacy and metacognitive awareness (48 high-school students, 2 camps).
- **S65** [research] Schoenfeld, "Explicit Heuristic Training as a Variable in Problem-Solving Performance" (1979) — https://pubs.nctm.org/view/journals/jrme/10/3/article-p173.xml — A short, explicitly taught heuristic list beat equal practice; generic labels too broad.
- **S66** [research] Sweller & Cooper, "The use of worked examples as a substitute for problem solving in learning algebra" (1985) — https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1207/s1532690xci0201_3 — Worked examples spare the working memory that means-ends search consumes.
- **S67** [research] Balban et al., "Brief structured respiration practices enhance mood and reduce physiological arousal" (2023) — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36630953/ — Daily cyclic sighing improved mood over a month; no acute performance outcome.
- **S68** [research] Richards & Gross, "Emotion regulation and memory: the cognitive costs of keeping one's cool" (2000) — https://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/releases/psp793410.pdf — Expressive suppression impaired verbal memory; reappraisal did not.
- **S69** [research] Wenzlaff & Wegner, "Thought Suppression" (2000) — https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev.psych.51.1.59 — Suppressing thoughts produces rebound.
- **S70** [research] Wang, Hagger, Chatzisarantis, "Ironic effects of thought suppression: a meta-analysis" (2020) — https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1745691619898795 — 31 studies: rebound regardless of load, immediate enhancement under cognitive load.
- **S71** [research] Roediger & Karpicke, "Test-Enhanced Learning: Taking Memory Tests Improves Long-Term Retention" (2006) — https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x — Retrieval practice beat re-study at one week (61% vs 40%) while re-study inflated confidence.
- **S72** [research] Rowland, "The effect of testing versus restudy on retention: A meta-analytic review of the testing effect" (2014) — https://doi.org/10.1037/a0037559 — Testing effect confirmed across 61 studies; recall beats recognition; emerges at longer delays.
- **S73** [research] Rohrer & Taylor, "The shuffling of mathematics problems improves learning" (2007) — http://uweb.cas.usf.edu/~drohrer/pdfs/Rohrer&Taylor2007IS.pdf — Interleaved practice improved one-week test performance sharply despite harder practice.
- **S74** [research] Brunmair & Richter, "Similarity matters: A meta-analysis of interleaved learning and its moderators" (2019) — https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000209 — Interleaving g = .42 overall, larger for similar categories, negative for words.
- **S75** [research] Oudejans & Pijpers, "Training with anxiety has a positive effect on expert perceptual–motor performance under pressure" (2009) — https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/17470210802557702 — Experts who practised under induced anxiety did not deteriorate under anxiety (n = 17 per experiment).
- **S76** [research] Oudejans & Pijpers, "Training with mild anxiety may prevent choking under higher levels of anxiety" (2010) — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1469029209000648 — Novices trained with mild anxiety held performance under high anxiety.
- **S77** [research] arXiv preprint (authors not recorded in R3), "How do Software Engineering Candidates Prepare for Technical Interviews?" (2025) — https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02068 — Survey (n = 131): few mocks; communication rehearsal and practising with others predicted perceived preparedness, hours and LeetCode counts did not.
- **S78** [research] Macnamara, Hambrick, Oswald, "Deliberate practice and performance in music, games, sports, education, and professions: A meta-analysis" (2014) — https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0956797614535810 — Deliberate practice explains little variance outside games, music and sports.
- **S79** [research] Cowan, "The magical number 4 in short-term memory" (2001) — https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X01003922 — Working-memory focus holds about four chunks; 7±2 was a rhetorical estimate.
- **S80** [research] Degani & Wiener, "Cockpit checklists: Concepts, design, and use" (1993) — https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/001872089303500209 — Long checklists get skipped, chunking relies on memory for order, interruptions break sequence, critical items go first.
- **S81** [research] Arriaga et al., "Simulation-based trial of surgical-crisis checklists" (2013) — https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMsa1204720 — Crisis checklists cut missed critical steps from 23% to 6% in simulated OR emergencies.
- **S83** [research] Scruggs & Mastropieri, "Mnemonic instruction for students with learning disabilities: What it is and what it does" (1990) — https://simvilledev.ku.edu/sites/default/files/researc/SC32Scruggs.Mnemonic%20instruction%20for%20students.1990.pdf — Acronyms work only for familiar targets and rarely encode order.
